# Anti-AI-slop writing

Write the way a careful human expert writes for a reader they respect:
**specific facts in plain words.** Chat
models drift toward the statistically safe sentence, so a vague, positive claim
replaces the specific fact. Every sign below is a symptom of that drift. The
cure for nearly all of them is the same: find the fact behind the phrase and
write the fact.

## How to write

1. **Before drafting**, gather the specifics: names, numbers, dates, places,
   quotes, what the thing actually does. Prose with no specifics to carry turns
   into slop however it is phrased.
2. **Draft** in plain words, using the "Write instead" column below.
3. **Sweep the draft** against every table below, sign by sign. Rewrite each
   hit. A rewrite that swaps one banned phrase for its neighbour in the same
   row is still a hit.
4. **Done** when a full sweep finds nothing. When editing someone else's text,
   the same sweep applies to every sentence you touch, and the change note
   follows the "Edit notes" table.

Quotes and code keep their own wording.

## Content

| Sign | Banned forms | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Puffed-up significance | stands as, serves as, is a testament/reminder, plays a crucial/pivotal/vital/key role, underscores its importance, reflects broader trends, enduring/lasting legacy, setting the stage for, marks a shift/turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted | The fact. Let the reader judge whether it matters. |
| Coverage padding | independent coverage, featured/profiled in, local/national media outlets, trade publications, active social media presence, a leading expert | The named source and what it said, or nothing. |
| Superficial -ing analysis | a sentence ending ", highlighting / underscoring / emphasizing / ensuring / reflecting / symbolizing / contributing to / fostering / showcasing / enhancing ..."; valuable insights; aligns/resonates with | End the sentence at the fact. A real effect gets its own sentence and its evidence. |
| Promotional tone | boasts, vibrant, rich history/heritage, profound, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, diverse array, world-class, cutting-edge, seamless, state-of-the-art | What it is and does, with numbers or names. |
| Vague association | in connection with, in association with, is associated/connected with | The actual relationship: founded, owns, wrote, funded, sued, married. |
| Weasel attribution | experts argue, observers note, industry reports, some critics, several sources/studies (when one or two are cited), it is widely believed | Who said it, cited. Or state it as fact if it is one. |
| Challenges outro | Despite its X, it faces several challenges...; Despite these challenges; Challenges and legacy; Future outlook | A real problem, with specifics, where it belongs in the text. No closing outlook section. |
| Generic statement | any sentence that would be true of a hundred similar subjects | The specific fact behind it. |
| Fabricated citation | a link, DOI, ISBN or page number you did not open and check | Only sources you opened, which say what the sentence says. |

## Language

| Sign | Banned forms | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rule of three** | any three-item series in running prose: three adjectives ("fast, friendly and affordable"), three phrases, three short sentences in a row ("Built in 1902. Burned in 1910. Rebuilt in 1912."), three parallel clauses | As many items as the facts have; two strong items beat three padded ones. When there really are three or more concrete items, use a bulleted list. |
| AI vocabulary | additionally, align with, bolster, crucial, deep dive, delve, enduring, enhance, foster, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, landscape (abstract), meticulous, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant, leverage, multifaceted, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally, dependable, echoed, clearer, practical, "this matters", key (as filler), challenge (as filler), causal, empirical, correlate | The plain word: has, is, shows, uses, also, important, careful, strong. |
| Copula avoidance | serves as / stands as / functions as / acts as a; features / offers / boasts / maintains a; represents a; refers to | is, are, has, have. |
| Negative parallelism | not just X but also Y; it's not X, it's Y; isn't about X, it's about Y; Y rather than X; no X, no Y, just Z | Say what it is. Mention what it is not only when a reader actually believes that. |
| Stiff synonyms | utilize, authored, relocated, attempted, passed away, commence, facilitate, endeavor | use, wrote, moved, tried, died, start, help, try. |
| Elegant variation | a new label for the same subject in each sentence | One name for one thing, every time. |
| Hedging disclaimers | it's important/worth noting; while specific details are limited; not widely documented; based on available information; in the provided sources; does not by itself establish; should not be confused with; as of my last knowledge update; as an AI language model | What is known, cited. Leave out what is not known; never announce its absence. |
| Summary endings | In summary, In conclusion, Overall, Ultimately, To sum up; a Conclusion or Key takeaways section; a closing line restating the paragraph | End on the last fact. |
| Rhythm overcorrection | every sentence 8 to 20 words, chopped with periods and commas where a dash used to be | Sentence length that follows the thought: some long, some short. |

## Formatting

| Sign | Banned forms | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Em dashes | — and spaced – or -- | A comma, colon, parentheses or a new sentence. |
| Title case headings | "The Rich History Of Our Town" | Sentence case: "History of the town". |
| "X and Y" headings | Awards and recognition, Challenges and legacy, History and background | One subject per heading; fold recognition into the history it belongs to. |
| Bold scattered in prose | several bolded "key terms" in one paragraph | At most one bold phrase per paragraph, for a term being defined. |
| Inline-header lists | `- **Location:** central`, `1. Historical Context:` | Bullets that are plain sentences, or prose. |
| Emoji as formatting | emoji before headings or bullets, sign-offs like "Happy editing! 🚀" | None. |
| Tiny tables | a two- or three-row table holding what one sentence says | A sentence. Tables only for real rows and columns. |
| Heading errors | a heading repeating the page title, skipped levels, many top-level headings, a heading holding only subheadings | One title, and heading levels in order. |
| Wrong markup | Markdown asterisks, `#` headings or `---` rules in HTML, wikitext, email or plain text | The destination's own markup. |
| Curly quotes | “ ” ‘ ’, especially mixed with straight ones | Straight quotes throughout, unless the publication's style requires curly ones everywhere. |
| Title as a proper noun | "**Catchment area (health)** refers to..."; "X editions is the chronological list of..." | Open with the subject itself: "A catchment area is...". |

## Talking to the reader

| Sign | Banned forms | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chatbot talk | I hope this helps; Of course!; Certainly!; You're absolutely right; Would you like me to...; Is there anything else; let me know; here is a...; great question; feel free to; a more detailed breakdown; check this before publishing | Nothing. The text speaks to its reader, never to whoever asked for it. |
| Form-letter openers | I hope this message finds you well; I am writing to...; Dear [Team]; I understand your concerns | Open with the point. |
| Customer-service tone | I have carefully reviewed; I apologize for any inconvenience; please point them out to me and I will resolve them | Plain statements of what was done and what is wrong. |
| Placeholders | [Your Name], [Year], [Insert link], (Add your URL here), 2025-XX-XX, INSERT_SOURCE_URL, PASTE_VIDEO_URL_HERE, example.com links | The real value, or cut the line. |
| Citation debris | utm_source=chatgpt.com / openai / copilot.com, referrer=grok.com, contentReference[oaicite:1], ↩ | Clean links, each opened and checked. |

## Edit notes

When the edit comes with a summary (a change note, changelog, commit message
or reply saying what changed):

| Sign | Banned forms | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canned assurance | refined, enhanced, enriched, streamlined, improved for clarity/flow/readability, ensured that it adheres to, neutral/encyclopedic/professional tone | What changed in the content, specifically: "cut the founding-date claim, which no source supports". |
| What was kept or avoided | while preserving the original meaning, retained the technical details, avoided... | Nothing. Describe only what changed. |
| Source boasting | added sourced/verified information, improved attribution, with independent secondary sources | The facts added, and which source supports each. |
| Markup detail | lists of parameter names, "internal links", "inline citations" | The reader-visible change. |
