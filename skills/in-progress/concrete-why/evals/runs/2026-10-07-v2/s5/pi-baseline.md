**Short answer: it's a deliberate, documented, lint-enforced decision — `docs/adr/0003-a-mutation-returns-its-change.md`, enforced by `plugins/nro-codestyle/rules/ban-a-list-of-callers-to-tell-later.rule.mjs`.** `GrantedTagRemoval` is not ceremony around an event; it *is* the notification, delivered as a value to the one caller who knows why the tag came off.

Why not `OnGameplayTagAdded`-style subscription, per the ADR and the code:

**1. Subscriptions have a startup window and no compile-time link.** The ADR's exact scenario: spawn setup grants `Stunned`, the health bar subscribes its own init a frame later, the bar never greys out — and only when spawn ordering shifts. Deleting a subscriber still compiles; dropping the returned `GrantedTagRemoval` does not (`perfect-typescripter/unconsumed-outcome`), so a missed reaction fails the build instead of failing silently on someone else's machine.

**2. `RemoveGrantedTag` runs in the middle of other mutations — listeners would fire into half-updated state.** A removal is triggered *from inside* an apply: `GameplayTagContainer.ts` calls `RemoveGrantedTag` at lines 577/591/678/721 while a grant is being processed (blocker arrived, exclusion-group pushout), and `TagExclusionGroupMembership.ts:97` reaches into *another* container mid-apply. The ADR's example: granting fire immunity removes burning while the immunity grant is still half-applied. A "burning removed" listener firing there could grant something else and recurse into a table that hasn't bumped `tagVersion` yet. The returned value comes back only after the container is consistent, so the caller reacts "in the order written here" (`journal.Record(taken); screen.TagStandingChanged(...)`).

**3. The value carries evidence a container event can't.** `TagRemovalReason` is authored by the caller who knows the story (`Cleansed`, `BlockerArrived`, `PushedOutOfExclusionGroup`…) — that's what lets a screen say *"their crawl ended, so Crawling came off"* (`AppliedTag.Types.ts`). The container doesn't know why; the remover does. And `GrantedTagRemoval`'s `NotActive / AlreadyRemoved` variant makes double-removal a reportable, testable outcome (`GameplayTagContainer.unit.ts:166`); under events, the event simply doesn't fire and nobody notices.

**4. Prediction would fire phantom events.** `PredictedFacts.Client.ts` reconciles by throwing away predicted state and replaying: `rebuildParticipantFacts` + `ReconstituteFromAgreed` (GameplayTagContainer.ts:623) re-apply every grant each time the server's account or new numbers arrive. With listeners, every reconcile would emit added/removed for changes the player never saw happen — the HUD would flicker through states that were only ever bookkeeping.

**5. The wiring growth is the same, just type-checked.** Per the ADR: one owner performs the mutation and tells its constructor-injected collaborators. Adding an applier touches no reader; adding a reader is one constructor parameter in one place — same as a subscription list grows, but with no runtime registration and no silent misses. And the callers show the pattern already: `EffectGrantsAuthority`, `EffectTagHolds`, `GameplayAbilitySetAuthority` collect `GrantedTag` receipts and hand each grant back when its cause ends.

Where NRO *does* subscribe: at the replication boundary (`ClientResourceBars.Client.ts` keeps a `Listeners` set) — that's the ADR's explicit exemption, because a network arrival is a Roblox signal with no call to return a value into. Gameplay mutation is a call you made, so it answers you directly.

Also worth noting: the project's own rules ban the alternative outright — AGENTS.md's "Direct transition fanout" forbids event buses and dynamic listener lists, and "Point at the thing, never at its name" is why the removal carries the `GrantedTag` handle (the exact seat) rather than a tag ID a subscriber pattern would key on.
